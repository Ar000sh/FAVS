Overview

Five aggregates in three contexts

Identity is bought (Keycloak), and Activity and Notification only listen, so the tactical model lives in three contexts: Group, Expense and Settlement. No aggregate holds another aggregate as an object. Each keeps IDs and learns about changes through events (slide 67, rules 3 and 4).

Before modelling · requirements §8

Decisions this model makes

The requirements leave four slide conflicts and ten domain questions open. The model needs answers to most of them, so here they are as proposals. Tick one off when the team agrees, then copy it into ShareWise_Requirements.md, which stays the single source of truth.

#ProposalWhyAgreed
Slide conflicts
D1Share is a value object. ExpenseParticipant (slide 64) is dropped.A share has no life of its own. A correction replaces the whole split, so nothing ever tracks one share over time, and two shares with the same participant and amount are interchangeable (slide 65). It matches the glossary: the participant is the member, the share is what they owe. Slide 64’s entity would only be right if a participant had its own state per expense, such as “paid back for this expense”. We settle per group.
D2At least 1 participant, and at least one participant who is not the payer (new rule R3a).“Alice pays Bob’s museum ticket” has one participant (Bob) but involves two people, which is what slide 64’s 2..* is after. An expense whose only participant is the payer changes no balance, so it is refused.
D3Expense and Settlement store MemberId, not UserId.Inside a group the person is a member, not an account (slide 40). The core stays independent of the external Identity model (ACL in the context map). Former members keep their MemberId (R13), and a placeholder member (J17) would have a MemberId without a UserId.
D4“Payer belongs to the group” is checked inside Expense, against a GroupRoster snapshot.Expense only holds a GroupId (rule 3). The application service loads a roster (active members, admins, open or not, currency) and passes it into record(), correct() and remove(). The roster is the Expense context’s own copy, fed by Group events (ACL, rule 4). In Ex4 its adapter may simply call Group’s public API; the domain code stays the same when it becomes an event-fed copy in P3.
Model choices
D5BalanceSheet (one per group) is an aggregate, not only a read model. New glossary term.AllBalancesSettled is a domain event, and domain events come from an aggregate’s public interface (slide 71). Real decisions depend on balances: leaving (R11), the payment limit (R17) and archiving (L5). “The sum is always 0” (R14) must hold after every change, which is exactly what an aggregate protects (rule 1).
D6Invitation is its own aggregate. Members live inside Group.An invitation has its own lifecycle and expires on a timer. Inside Group, every invite would lock the whole group (rule 2). Members must stay inside Group because “the last admin can’t leave” (R12) has to see all admins in one transaction.
D7The settlement plan is computed on demand and never stored.It is derived from the current balances, so it can’t go stale after a partial payment or a reopen. This answers “plan recalculation” in §8.
Domain questions from §8
D8Auto-archive only from CLOSED_FOR_SETTLEMENT.In an open group the balances can reach 0 mid-trip, for example when everyone has paid back on day 2. Archiving then would end the trip. BalanceSheet raises AllBalancesSettled only in the settlement phase, and Group checks its own status again.
D9An expense that involves a former member can’t be corrected or removed (new rule R7a). If everyone involved is still a member, a correction after settlement payments is fine: balances move, and the plan is recalculated.Bob left at balance 0 (R11). Changing his share afterwards would give a non-member a debt nobody can settle. This answers “correction after a settlement payment” in §8.
D10A settlement payment may not exceed the debtor’s open debt or the creditor’s open credit (sharpens R17).Nobody flips from debtor to creditor, so balances always move toward 0 and AllBalancesSettled stays reachable. In an archived group every balance is 0, so no payment can be recorded there at all.
D11A rejected settlement payment stays REJECTED. The debtor records a new one.The lifecycle stays one-way (PENDING → CONFIRMED or REJECTED), and the history shows what really happened.
D12Any active member may invite, only while the group is OPEN. One invitation is for one person (an email or a personal link).J3 names a member (Alice) as the actor. The statuses ACCEPTED and DECLINED (R9) only make sense for one invitee. A reusable group link would be a separate Could feature.
D13A former member who is invited again becomes ACTIVE with the same MemberId.Their balance was 0 when they left. One MemberId keeps their history and their BalanceSheet entry together. The late-joiner rule (R10) applies again from the new join.

Still open, and the model doesn’t depend on them yet: deleting a group, GDPR account deletion, and placeholder members (J17) versus R9. D3 keeps J17 possible later.

Aggregates

An aggregate is a cluster of entities and value objects owned by one root, and it is a transactional consistency boundary (slide 67). Each card lists the commands it accepts with the events they publish, the invariants it protects, and the four aggregate rules as a checklist.

inside always true after the transaction snapshot checked against another aggregate’s data, which can be slightly old (rule 4)

Expense

Expense context · corethe slide-69 aggregate with the slide-64 conflicts resolved
Commands → events
  • record(roster, recorder, title, date, amount, payer, split) → ExpenseRecorded J4
  • correct(roster, by, newVersion, reason) → ExpenseCorrected, old version into history J5
  • remove(roster, by) → ExpenseRemoved J6
Invariants it protects
  • R1 The amount is positive.
  • R3 At least one participant, nobody twice.
  • R3a At least one participant is not the payer (D2).
  • R4 The shares add up exactly to the amount; guaranteed by Split.
  • G2 The amount is in the group’s currency.
  • R7 A correction moves the old version into the history; nothing is overwritten.
  • R8 A removed expense can’t be corrected; REMOVED is final.
  • R2 R3 Payer and participants are active members (snapshot).
  • R5 L2 L3 Only while the group is OPEN (snapshot).
  • R6 Only the recorder, the payer or an admin corrects or removes (snapshot).
  • R7a No correction or removal if a former member is involved (snapshot, D9).

R10 (late joiners) needs no check of its own: it follows from R2 and R3, because a participant must be a member when the expense is recorded.

Aggregate rules (slide 67)
  • 1 · Invariants via the root. Split and Share can only be replaced through correct(). There is no setter for amount or shares.
  • 2 · Small. One expense, its shares and its few earlier versions. A “GroupExpenses” aggregate holding the whole list would make every new expense in a group wait for every other one.
  • 3 · By ID. GroupId and MemberId for recorder, payer and participants. No Group or Member objects.
  • 4 · Eventual. Balances change in Settlement after the event, in a separate transaction.
Changed from slide 64
  • ExpenseParticipant entity → Share value object (D1)
  • UserId → MemberId (D3)
  • 2..* participants → 1..* plus R3a (D2)
  • correct(newAmount) → correct(newVersion), because J5 also changes payer, participants and split (F5)

The revision list is a natural event-sourcing candidate (slide 72: an append-only history). For Ex4 a plain list is enough.

Invariants it protects
  • G1 The name is not empty (GroupName).
  • G2 Exactly one currency, fixed at creation.
  • L1 Only admins close, reopen, archive, remove and promote.
  • L6 Only allowed status transitions; ARCHIVED is final.

  • R13 Leaving or removal makes a member FORMER; nobody is deleted.

Aggregate rules (slide 67)
  • 1 · Invariants via the root. Role checks and transitions happen in Group’s methods. There is no updateStatus() (slide 35).
  • 2 · Small. Members (a few dozen at most) are inside because R12 needs all admins at once. Invitations, expenses and balances are not.
  • 3 · By ID. UserId (Identity) and the invitation’s ID. Nothing from Expense or Settlement.
  • 4 · Eventual. Expense and Settlement learn about members and status through events; archiving reacts to AllBalancesSettled.

Why the balance is a parameter: Group must not know how balances are calculated. The application service asks Settlement’s public interface (OHS) and hands the answer to leave(). The answer can be a few milliseconds old; see finding F7.

  • 1 · Invariants via the root. Status only changes through accept(), decline() and expire().
  • 2 · Small. One invitation for one invitee. It doesn’t contain the group.
  • 3 · By ID. GroupId, the inviter’s MemberId, the accepting UserId.

BalanceSheet

Settlement context · coreDecision D5 · new glossary term: the balances of all members of one group
Commands (each triggered by an event) → events
  • on GroupCreated: a new BalanceSheet for the group; on MemberJoined: addMember(m) at 0
  • on ExpenseRecorded: applyExpense(effect) credits the payer, debits each participant
  • on ExpenseCorrected: replaceExpense(before, after) reverts the old effect and applies the new one
  • on ExpenseRemoved: revertExpense(effect)
  • on SettlementPaymentConfirmed: applySettlementPayment(debtor, creditor, amount) → AllBalancesSettled if this was the last one
  • on GroupClosedForSettlement: startSettlementPhase() → AllBalancesSettled at once if every balance is already 0
  • on GroupReopened: endSettlementPhase()
Queries
  • balanceOf(member) for leaving and removal (R11)
  • openDebtOf() and openCreditOf() for the payment limit (R17, D10)
  • calculateFinalBalances() for the close summary, J12 step 2 (slide 35 name)
  • suggestSettlementPlan() → SettlementPlan (R15, J7)
Invariants it protects
  • R14 The sum of all balances is 0 after every change.
  • R14 Only confirmed settlement payments count; it only listens to …Confirmed.
  • G2 One currency.
  • L5 Raises AllBalancesSettled when the group is in its settlement phase and every balance is 0 (D8).
  • R15 Making a plan never changes a balance: the plan is computed and returned, not applied.
Aggregate rules (slide 67)
  • 1 · Invariants via the root. Balances change only through apply and revert methods. There is no setBalance().
  • 2 · Small. One Money per member. No expense list and no payment list; it keeps only their net effect.
  • 3 · By ID. GroupId and MemberId. It never sees an Expense object, only the ExpenseEffect its ACL builds from the event.
  • 4 · Eventual. It is updated by events from Expense and SettlementPayment, and its own event archives the Group.

(note:) Every event must be applied exactly once. The event-listener adapter remembers which event IDs it has processed (an “inbox”). This becomes important in P3, when RabbitMQ may deliver an event twice.

Review passes

Review findings

Reviewing the model against the requirements turned up places where the requirements contradict themselves or can’t be built as written. Each finding has a proposed fix for ShareWise_Requirements.md, once the team agrees.

fix the wordingF1 · R14

“Active expenses only” would drop corrected expenses

R14 counts “active expenses only”, but a corrected expense has status CORRECTED, and the corrected dinner (€200) clearly still counts in the Mallorca balances. Taken literally, the code would ignore every corrected expense.

Fix: “over all expenses that are not removed”.

fix the wordingF2 · R15

“Minimum number of payments” can’t be promised

(note:) Finding the true minimum is NP-hard: it means splitting the members into as many zero-sum groups as possible. The usual greedy plan (largest debtor pays largest creditor) needs at most one payment fewer than the number of members with a non-zero balance, but not always the minimum. Script check: balances +6, +4, −3, −3, −4 give 4 greedy payments, while 3 are possible ({+4, −4} and {+6, −3, −3}). For Mallorca, greedy and minimum are both 3.

Fix: “The settlement plan needs at most n − 1 payments for n members with a non-zero balance. It never changes anyone’s balance.”

clarifyF3 · R17

“Open debt” has two readings

It can mean the debt to this creditor in the plan, or the debtor’s whole negative balance. Two pending payments can also add up to more than the debt.

Fix: D10. Check against open debt and open credit when recording, and again when confirming.

clarifyF4 · R7 vs R11

Corrections can reopen a former member’s balance

Bob left at balance 0. Correcting the hotel afterwards would give him a share again, so a non-member would owe money.

Fix: new rule R7a (D9).

noteF5 · slide 64

correct(newAmount) is too narrow

J5 lets you change the payer, the participants and the split too.

Fix: correct(roster, by, newVersion, reason). Say in P1 that we deliberately extended the slide.

noteF6 · R10

R10 needs no code of its own

A participant must be a member when the expense is recorded (R2, R3), so late joiners can’t end up in older expenses. The recording time decides, not the expense date: an expense dated day 1 but recorded on day 4 may include Eve. That matches R10’s wording.

discuss in P1F7 · rule 4

Eventual consistency leaves small windows

An expense can be recorded in the same moment the group is closed, and a member can leave while an expense that includes them is being saved. In the monolith the roster updates right after Group commits, so the window is milliseconds. We accept it (slide 67, rule 4). If a former member ever ends up with a non-zero balance, Settlement can alert an admin. That kind of compensation is what Saga in P3 is about.

noteF8 · §7

Two listed events aren’t ours

UserRegistered has no subscriber, because Group takes the UserId from the login token. PaymentReminderSent changes no state, so it is a Notification command. Neither is modelled as our domain event in the MVP.

clarifyF9 · J3

An “invite link” can mean two things

A personal link for one invitee, or one group link many people can use. The statuses ACCEPTED and DECLINED only fit the first.

Fix: D12. One invitation is for one person; a reusable link is Could.
